>[!definition]
>Electric Current is the rate of flow of electric charge (per unit time)
> #PhysicsDefinitions 

>[!formulae]
>$I = \frac{Q}{t}$$
>where I = current (A)
>Q = charge (C)
>t = time taken (s) 
>
>Another variation: 
>$I = \frac{P}{V}$
>where P is power in watt


SI unit for current = Ampere (A) or Coulomb (C) per second (s) 
- 1 ampere -> is the electric current produced when 1 coulomb of charge passes a point in 1 second

1 coulomb is not equal to 1 electron

>[!note]
>Coulomb -> derived unit. 
>Ampere -> SI based unit
>1 coulomb = amount of charge when 1 ampere of current -> flows for 1 second. 
>1 coulomb -> approx. equal to charge of $6.25 \times 10^{18}$ protons. 
>charge on each proton -> is about $1.60 \times 10^{-19}C$

>[!formulae]
>Additional formulae:
>Rate of flow of electrons along wire. 
>$\frac{I}{charge\ on\ electron}$
>flows in opposite direction to the current. conventional current is opposite to electron flow

>[!formulae]
>Additional Formulae: 
>Formula for charge
>$Q = number\ of\ electrons\ flowing \times charge\ of\ one\ electron (1.6 \times 10^{-19})$
>When you add this to the current formula:
>$I = \frac{number\ of\ electrons\ flowing \times charge\ of\ one\ electron}{time}$

>[!understanding]
>A **dry cell** is a single electrochemical cell with a **paste electrolyte**, typically producing **1.5V** (e.g., zinc-carbon, alkaline cells). A **battery** consists of **two or more cells** connected in **series** (to increase voltage) or **parallel** (to increase capacity). In **O-Level Physics**, the total **EMF in series** is the sum of individual EMFs, while in **parallel**, it remains the same as one cell.
