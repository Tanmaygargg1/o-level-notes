#### Different Attraction Of Electrons By Different Atoms
- Atoms of diff. Elements -> diff. Characteristics -> due to different compositions
- Electrons of some atoms -> held more strongly by nuclei than electrons in other atoms

When 2 objects (e.g. balloon and hair) come into contact:
-<mark style="background: #ADCCFFA6;"> Less tightly held electrons</mark> from hair -> are attracted and transferred to balloon
- Oppositely charged hair and balloon attract each other -> thus stick together
![[Pasted image 20250123211109.png|450]]

Since hair and balloon -> both insulators -> electrons cannot move freely throughout materials
- Electrons transferred to balloons surface -> will not be able to move -> elsewhere. 
- Similarly -> vacant electron sites in the hair -> cannot be refilled by other electrons in hair.
-> this is why they are called *static* electricity. 
![[Pasted image 20250123211247.png|300]]
<mark style="background: #FF5582A6;">Top is greater comparative tendency to lose electron 
Bottom is greater comparative tendency to gain electron </mark>
>[!important]
>both materials have to be insulators in this case, else wont remain charged

> [!note] 
> - example: rubbing leather and nylon together -> give them positive and negative charge respectively. 
> - for stronger electrostatic charging -> choose a pair of materials -> spaced further apart
> - in humid environment -> moisture on surfaces -> and in the air -> can conduct and lead to loss of static charges

> [!example]
> Electroscope charged using charged glass rod to touch top plate
> - however by placing rod just above top plate -> without touching -> we can also get gold leaf to be repelled. 
> 
> Positive charges in glass rod -> attract electrons from the plate, stem, gold leaf. 
> - causes electrons -> to accumulate -> in top plate. 
> - since plate, stem, leaf -> originally neutral -> movement of some electrons to top results in excess of positive charges at bottom. 
> - hence gold leaf is repelled.
