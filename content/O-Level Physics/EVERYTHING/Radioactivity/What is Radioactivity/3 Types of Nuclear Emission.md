3 types:
- alpha $\alpha$ particles
- beta $\beta$  particles
- gamma $\gamma$ rays

____
**ionisation** -> refers to ability to eject electrons from atoms to form ions.
since atoms lose electrons -> number of protons > number of electrons. 
- thus ions carry charge


| Nuclear Emission | Nature                                                                                                                   | Relative ionising Effect | Relative Penetrating Ability                                                                                                |
| ---------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ | ------------------------ | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
|                  | an $\alpha$ particle -> consists of 2 protons and 2 neutrons tightly bound together.<br> <br>identical to helium nucleus | highest                  | - least <br>- easily absorbed by piece of paper, thin aluminium foil, human skin                                            |

nuclei of same isotope will emit same type of nuclear radiation. 
during $\alpha$-decay or $\beta$-decay -> nucleus changes to that of diff. element

___
### Alpha Decay
when a nucleus undergoes $\alpha$-decay: 
- emits an $\alpha$ particle (identical to helium nucleus $^{4}_{2}He$ )
- nucleon number decreases by 4 and proton number decreases by 2

we can use equation involving nuclide notation to represent changes in composition of nucleus. 
known as nuclide equation.
e.g. for radium (Ra) emits $\alpha$ particle -> it decays to radon (Rn)
$^{226} _{88} Ra \rightarrow ^{226}_{86}Rn + ^{4}_{2}He$


arrow means -> react to form. 
total number of nucleons and protons before and after reaction is same

Nucleon numbers: 226 -> 222 + 4
Proton numbers: 88 -> 86 + 2

total relative charge before and after nuclear emission should be same
___
### Beta Decay
when nucleus undergoes $\beta$ decay:
- it emits a $\beta$ particle (an electron)
- nucleon number remains same and proton number increases by one

e.g. carbon-14 (radioactive carbon C) emits an $\beta$ particle (electron) it decays to nitrogen (N)

$^{14}_6 C \rightarrow ^{14}_7 N + ^0_{-1}e$

___
### Gamma Radiation
often when nuclei undergo alpha decay or beta decay -> gamma radiation is also emitted. 

e.g. when uranium emits alpha particle -> it decays to form Thorium and 2 gamma rays of diff. energies are emitted

$^{238}_{92} U \rightarrow ^{234}_{90} Th + ^4_2 He + 2^0_0 \gamma$
