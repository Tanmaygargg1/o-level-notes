>[!definition]
>Definition: refraction is the bending of light as it passes from one optical medium to another, due to difference in optical density of mediums*
#PhysicsDefinitions 

___
**Refracted Ray** -> is the light ray that enters a medium and undergoes change of direction.
<span style="color:rgb(0, 176, 240)">- angle of refraction is the angle between the refracted ray and the normal at the point of incidence. </span> 
Incident angle is labelled $i$ and angle of refraction is labelled $r$

#### Laws of Refraction
>[!law]
>**First Law:**
>First law of refraction states that the incident ray, refracted ray, and the normal at the point of incidence all lie in the same plane. 
> #PhysicsDefinitions 

>[!law]
>**Second Law:**
>Second law of refraction states that for 2 given media, the ratio of the sine of the angle of incidence to the sine of the angle of refraction is a constant, that is, $\frac{sin\ i}{sin\ r} = constant$ 
> #PhysicsDefinitions 
> #physicsFormulae 
> Note: that $n = \frac{c}{v}$ also returns refractive index, using speed of light in the different media. 

>[!law]
>**Snell's Law:**
>Snell's law -> relates angle of incidence to the angle of refraction. 
>$$n_{1}\sin(\theta_{i}) = n_{2}\sin(\theta_{r})$$
>Where $n_1$ and $n_2$ are the refractive indices of the first and second mediums, and $\theta_i$ and $\theta_r$ are angles of incidence and refraction respectively. 
> #PhysicsDefinitions 
> #physicsFormulae

>[!formulae]
>### All Formulas
>
>1. $n = \frac{c}{v}$
>2. $n = \frac{sin i}{sin R}$
>3. $n_1sin\theta_i = n_2sin\theta_r$
>	- where $\theta_1$ is angle of incidence and $\theta_2$ is angle of refraction
>	- $n_1$ is refractive index of first medium and $n_2$ the second
>1. $n  = \frac{1}{sin C}$
>2. $n = \frac{real \ depth}{apparent \ depth}$
>   
>>[!important]
>>- use 2 and 4 only when 1 of the media is vacuum or air. 
>>- all other cases use 3
>>- 4 is just a manipulation of 2 -> when sin C = $90\degree$
>>- Example: $n_1sin\theta_i = n_2sin 90$
>> 	 $\frac{n_g}{n_w} = \frac{1}{sin\theta_g}$
> 
> #physicsFormulae 

$n= \frac{c}{v}$ -> also returns the refractive index. 
- <mark style="background: #FFB8EBA6;">Where C is the speed of light in air, and v is the speed of light in a vacuum. </mark>
