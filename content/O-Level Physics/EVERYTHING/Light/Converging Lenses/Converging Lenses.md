*Device made of glass or other transparent material that is able to concentrate light rays (at a point) is called converging lens*

Example of where converging lenses are used: *Magnifying Glass*
![[Pasted image 20241206010313.png|240]]
-> made up of a type of glass called a **converging lens**
- its shape is thicker in the centre such that a beam of parallel light rays passing through it will be brought to a single focus point.
___
##### How does Converging Lens **Converge** parallel rays to a point
-> consider the lens to be made up of different section (like in the diagram)
- refraction happens upon entry and exit of each section. 
- All the emerging rays converge at a point.
![[Pasted image 20241206010544.png|300]]

> [!important]
> Effect of [[Refractive Index]] on focal length. E.g. if we say red light has lower refractive index than blue light
> - focal length for blue light would be shorter compared to red light
> - with larger refractive index for blue light, light would bend more towards normal when it enters glass, causing light to bend more away from the normal when it exits to air
> - resulting in shorter focal length

___
>[!important]
>**Cover Part of Converging Lens**
>- image is still formed but dimmer -> less light passes through the lens
>- every part of the lens contributes to the formation of the entire image -> so even if you block part of it, lens still focuses light from the uncovered section. 
>	- each small part of the lens bends light rays from ALL parts of the object, not just from a single section.
>- Position AND size of image do not change -> as long as object remains in same place
>- however since less light is contributing to image -> appear s less bright (possible less sharp)
>- focal length of lens remains same - focal length depends on curvature and material of lens, not amt of light passing through
>- if significant portion (more than half) then diffraction effects might start to affect image clarity (becomes blur) leading to distortion 

>[!sample]
>When they ask you what happens when converging lens is partially covered
>- darker/dimmer image compared xx -> greater/smaller surface area ->  more/less light can pass through 

>[!note]
>regardless of size of converging lens -> if focal length is same -> image will be in focus -> same focal length
