1. Liquid at A is exposed to atmosphere. Thus, pressure $P_A$ at A is the atmospheric pressure $P_{atm}$
2. When gas supply is turned on, gas will exert pressure $P_B$ on liquid at B. As $P_B$ is higher than the atmospheric pressure, liquid at A will rise until pressure $P_C$ at C, which is on the same horizontal level as B, equals the gas pressure. Pressure $P_C$ at C is given by $P_C - P_A = h\rho g$
3. Since $P_C = P_B$ and $P_A = P_{atm}$, we determine pressure $P_B$ of gas to be: 
	$P_B - P_{atm} = h\rho g$
	$P_B = h\rho g + P_{atm}$
