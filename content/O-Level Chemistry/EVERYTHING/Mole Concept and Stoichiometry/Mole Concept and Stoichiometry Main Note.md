Carbon-12: is taken as the standard atom. all other atoms are compared with this standard atom.

**Relative Atomic Mass**
>[!definition]
> Relative Atomic Mass $A_r$ of an element is the average mass of one atom of that element relative to $\frac{1}{12}$ the mass of an atom of carbon-12
#ChemistryDefinitions
  
Relative Atomic Mass = $\frac{average\ mass\ of\ one\ atom\ of\ the\ element}{\frac{1}{12} mass\ of\ an\ atom\ of\ carbon-12}$

Relative Atomic Mass is represented by $A_r$ and has NO unit
<mark style="background: #FFF3A3A6;">(found in periodic table)</mark>
___
**Calculating Relative Atomic Mass of elements with Isotopes**\
some elements found in nature as a mixture of isotopes ==> with different atomic masses
thus relative atomic mass if determined by the average atomic mass of the isotopes.

Eg: relative atomic mass of chlorine ==> given in decimal form.
-> this is because there are only 2 stable isotopes of chlorine - chlorine 35 and chorine 37
sample of chlorine usually has 75% chlorine-35 and 25% chlorine-37

Relative atomic mass of chlorine -> (75/100 * 35) + (25/100 * 37) = 35.5
___
**Relative Molecular Mass**
>[!definition]
>Relative molecular mass, $M_r$ of a molecular substance is the average mass of one molecule of that substance relative to $\frac{1}{12}$ the mass of an atom of carbon-12
#ChemistryDefinitions 

Relative molecular mass of covalent substance = $\frac{average\ mass\ of\ a\ molecule\ of\ the\ covalent\ substance}{\frac{1}{12}mass\ of\ an\ atom\ of\ carbon-12}$

Relative molecular mass is represented by $M_r$ and has NO unit

To find relative molecular mass $M_r$ of a substance, we add together the relative atomic mass $A_r$ of all the atoms in its chemical formula.
*Example:*
* $NH_3$ => $M_r$ = (1 $\times$ 14) + (3 $\times$ 1) = 17
___
**Relative Formula Mass**
Ionic compounds do not exist as molecules (they are made up of cations and anions)
we use the term relative formula mass to refer to the relative mass of an ionic compound.

>[!definition]
>Relative formula mass $M_r$ of an ionic compound is the average mass of 1 unit of that compound relative to $\frac{1}{12}$ the mass of an atom of carbon-12
#ChemistryDefinitions 

=> relative formula mass of ionic compound is also represented by $M_r$ and has NO unit
<mark style="background: #ABF7F7A6;">=> calculated in the same way as relative molecular mass.</mark>

## Mole and Molar Mass

**What is Mole**
=> Amount of substance containing $6.02 \times 10^{23}$ Particles
(One Mole represents $6.02 \times 10^{23}$ Particles)

Unit of Mole -> mol

Recall => 6 main types of particles. 
* atoms
* molecules
* ions
* subatomic particles
	* electrons
	* protons
	* neutrons
=> note that one mole of any substance will only have the specified number of particles => regardless of whether there are atoms, molecules, ions, and even sub atomic particles such as electrons, protons, neutrons. 
*Example*
1 mol of Helium (He) => has 1 mol of He Atoms => and that is $6.02 \times 10^{23}$

**Formula for Mole**
$$Number of Moles = \frac{Number\ of\ Particles}{6.02 \times 10^{23}(Avogadros\ Constant )}$$
![[Screenshot 2024-08-20 at 2.22.25 PM 2.png]]
<mark style="background: #ABF7F7A6;">Note: this value is also known as the Avogadros constant</mark>

* Do NOT present working and final answers in the form of fractions but decimals
* when dealing with non exact numerical final answers (with decimals) => correct value to 3 significant figures. Work with 5 S.F beforehand.

## Molar Mass

>[!definition]
>One Mole of Any substance has a mass equal to its relative atomic mass ($A_r$), relative molecular mass ($M_r$) or relative formula mass ($M_r$) in grams
This is the Molar Mass
><mark style="background: #D2B3FFA6;">Molar mass is the mass of 1 mole of any substance*</mark>
> #ChemistryDefinitions 

unit: g/mol

**Calculation of Molar Mass of an element**
* Molar mass of an element is the mass of one mole of atoms (or molecules) of the element
* since molar mass of element is equal to relative atomic mass ($A_r$) or relative molecular mass ($M_r$) => calculation of molar mass of an element is the same as the way you would do it to calculate its $A_r$ or $M_r$

**Calculation of Molar Mass of a Compound**
* Molar Mass of compound is the mass of one mole of molecules (or formula units) of the compound
* Molar Mass of compound is equal to its relative molecular mass ($M_r$) or relative formula mass ($M_r$)
* thus => calculate the molar mass of compound in the same way you would calculate its $M_r$

*Formula 1:*
$$Number\ of\ moles\ of\ a\ substance = \frac{mass\ of\ substance\ in\ g}{molar\ mass\ of\ substance\ in\ g/mol}$$
=> Mass must be in grams (convert if others)
=> Do not present working and final answers in form of fraction
=> give non exact final answers to 3S.F

## Mole and Molar Volume of Gasses
-> According to Avogadro's Law => equal volumes of all gasses => under the same conditions of temperature and pressure => contain the same number of particles.

>[!definition]
>Volume occupied by 1 mole of any gas at room temperature and pressure is equal to $24dm^3$
> #ChemistryDefinitions 

1dm = 10cm
$1dm^3$ => 1000$cm^3$

*Deduce Relationship between number of moles of gas and volume of gas*
* Equal number of mol of any gas occupies the same volume

*Formula 1*
$$ Number\ of\ moles\ of\ gas(mol)= \frac{volume\ of\ gas(dm^3)}{molar\ volume\ of\ gas (24dm^3)}$$
=> volume must be in $dm^3$ (conversion required)
1dm3 = 1000cm3
1l = 1000ml = 1000cm3 = 1dm3
=> do not present working and final answer in fraction
=> give non exact numerical final answers correct to 3s.f.

## Empirical and Molecular Formula of a Compound
>[!definition]
Simplest ration of elements in a compound is called its empirical formula
(Empirical formula is the simplest formula of a compound)
#ChemistryDefinitions 

=> Molecular formula is a multiple of the empirical formula
	-> molecular formula of a compound reflects the exact number of atoms of each element found in one molecule of the compound

Molecular formula/chemical formula => are sometimes used interchangeably.
	-> However the term 'molecular' -> only refers to covalent substances. 
			-> hence the term molecular formula refers only to the chemical formula of covalent substance

<span style="color:rgb(0, 176, 240)">Note: Some elements have the same empirical formula and molecular formula</span>
![[Screenshot 2024-08-20 at 2.22.25 PM 3.png|500]]

*What does empirical formula of a compound show* 
* elements in a compound
* the simplest ratio of the different types of atoms (elements) in the compounds

**The Molecular Formula is the actual formula of a compound**

*What does Molecular Formula of a compound show*
* elements in a compound
* the Actual Number of the different types of atoms (elements) in the compound

**Calculating Empirical Formula using mass of elements**
compound. Y contains 39.1g of carbon, 8.7g of hydrogen and 52.2g of oxygen. calculate the empirical formula of Y.

Steps:
1. find mass
2. find molar mass/ $A_r$
3. no. of moles/mol
4. $\div$ by smallest ratio
5. Obtain simplest ratio

| Element                                                 | C                        | H                         | O                        |
| ------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------ | ------------------------- | ------------------------ |
| mass/g                                                  | 39.1                     | 8.7                       | 52.2                     |
| molar mass/g/mol<br><br>(OR relative atomic mass/$A_r$) | 12                       | 1                         | 16                       |
| No. of Moles/mol                                        | $\frac{39.1}{12} = 3.26$ | $\frac{8.7}{1} = 8.7$     | $\frac{52.2}{16} = 3.26$ |
| $\div$ by smallest no.                                  | $\frac{3.26}{3.26} = 1$  | $\frac{8.7}{3.26} = 2.67$ | $\frac{3.26}{3.26} = 1$  |
| Simplest Ratio (Make it a whole number)                 | $1 \times 3$ <br><br>3:  | $2.67 \times 3$<br><br>8: | $1 \times 3$<br><br>3    |
Simplest Ratio: 3:8:3
(recall percentage of each compound is directly proportional to its mass in grams. Thus, mass of each element in 100g of the compound is its % in the compound.)
(Recall molecular formula is the formula that shows the exact number of atoms of each element in a molecule)

The molecular formula is a multiple of the empirical formula if the molecular formula and the empirical formula of a compound is different.

Thus to determine molecular formula we need to know the:
* Empirical formula of the compound
* Relative molecular mass of the compound
$$n = \frac{relative\ molecular\ mass}{M_r\ from\ empirical\ formula}$$

Molecular formula of a compound = $n\ \times empirical\ formula\ of\ a\ compound$
(from this, we can tell that molecular formula is a multiple of the empirical formula)

## Calculations from Chemical Reactions

what important info does a balanced chemical equation tell us:
* mole ratio => the ratio of the number of moles of the reactants and the products
* Physical state of the reactants and products

*What is stoichiometry*
=> relationship between number of moles of the reactants and the number of moles of the products involved in that chemical reaction.
=> **Stoichiometry** is based on the fact that particles in matter cannot be destroyed or created in a chemical reaction. in a chemical reaction, the particles in reactants simply changed into another form in the products. 
thus, sum of mass of reactants is the same as sum of mass of products.

=> consider the reaction between nitrogen gas and hydrogen gas

| $N_2(g)$ +                          | $3H_2(g)$ +                      | $2NH_3(g)$                         |
| ----------------------------------- | -------------------------------- | ---------------------------------- |
| 1 molecule $N_2$<br>1 mol $N_2$<br> | 3 molecules $H_2$<br>3 mol $H_2$ | 2 molecules $NH_3$<br>2 mol $NH_3$ |
-> this balanced equation can be read as 1 mol of nitrogen gas reacts with 3 mol of hydrogen gas to produce 2 mol of ammonia gas.

| No. of moles of $N_2$ = 1 mol                       | No. of moles of $H_2$ = 3 mol                     | No. of moles of $NH_3$  = 2 mol                         |
| --------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------- |
| Mass of $N_2$<br>= 1 mol $\times$ (2 * 14)<br>= 28g | Mass of $H_2$ = 3 mol $\times$ (2* 1)<br>= 6g<br> | Mass of $NH_3$ = 2 mol $\times$ [14 + (3 * 1)]<br>= 34g |
| Total mass of reactants = 28 + 6 = 34g              | Total mass of reactants = 28 + 6 = 34g            | Total mass of product = 34g                             |

### Calculations from Chemical Reactions
- **Titration in volumetric analysis**
- **Calculating Concentration of aqueous solutions of reactants and products**

*Volumetric analysis*
=> titration is a method used to prepare some soluble salts.
=> titration can also be used to determine concentration of an aqueous solution by reacting it with a solution of known concentration (standard solution).
known as volumetric analysis

Titration involves:
* Solution with unknown concentration (titre)
* Solution with known concentration (titrant)

<span style="color:rgb(255, 192, 0)">Example: Acid base titration  => we can find out volume of titrant (alkali) required to react with fixed volume of titre (acid with unknown concentration)</span>

Steps:
1. Pipette 25.0$cm^3$ of acid (titre) into a conical flask
2. Add a few drops of suitable indicator eg: methyl orange
3. Fill the burette with the alkali (titrant). Measure and record the initial burette reading. 
4. Run the alkali into the conical flask, swirling the flask in the process (to ensure proper mixing of reaction mixture)
5. Titration is stopped at the end point, i.e. when indicator has changed color permanently. 
6. Measure and record final burette reading. calculate volume of alkali (titrant) added
7. Titration is repeated until consistent results are obtained.
8. Average titrant volume is calculated
9. Volume is used to calculate unknown concentration of the solution

## Calculations from Chemical Reactions
- **Calculate the amount of product formed in a reaction with limiting reactant**

*What is a Limiting Reactant*
* is used up in a chemical reaction. when it is used up, reaction stops
*What is a Reactant in Excess*
* Not used up in reaction so at the end of the reaction there is some left
*Hence, state importance of limiting reactant*
* quantity of product obtained is decided by quantity of limiting reactant

##### Worked Example 1
when 6g of hydrogen is allowed to react with 32g of oxygen, water is produced
i) identify limiting reactant in this reaction
ii) calculate mass of water formed

<mark style="background: #D2B3FFA6;">i)</mark>
*Step 1*: write balanced chemical equation for this reaction (include state symbols)
$2H_2(g) + O_2(g) \rightarrow 2H_2O(l)$

*Step 2:* Calculate number of moles of reactants provided for this reaction
no. of moles of $H_2$ provided for the reaction = $\frac{6}{1\times2}$ = 3 mol
no. of moles of $O_2$ provided for the reaction = $\frac{32}{2\times16}$ = 1 mol

*Step 3:* Determine limiting reactant by calculating the actual amount of each reactant required for complete reaction using mole ratio

| Method 1                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                               | Method 2                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                  |
| -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Compare mole ratio, <br>$\frac{no.\ of\ moles\ of\ O_2}{no.\ of\ moles\ of\ H_2} = \frac{1}{2}$ <br><br>No. of moles of $O_2$ required:<br>= $\frac{1}{2} \times 3$ <br>= 1.5 mol<br><br>1.5 mol of $O_2$ is required to react completely => with 3 mol of $H_2$ <br>Since => only 1 mol of $O_2$ is provided for this reaction, $O_2$ is the limiting reactant and $H_2$ is in excess | Compare mole ratio.<br>$\frac{no.\ of\ moles\ of\ H_2}{no.\ of\ moles\ of\ O_2} = \frac{2}{1}$ <br><br>No. of moles of $H_2$ required<br>= $\frac{2}{1} \times 1$ <br>= 2 mol<br><br>2 mol of $H_2$ is required to react completely with 1 mol of $O_2$. <br>since => 3 mol of $H_2$ is provided for this reaction, $H_2$ is in excess and $O_2$ is the limiting reactant |
|                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                        |                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                           |
<mark style="background: #D2B3FFA6;">ii)</mark>
*Use limiting reactant to calculate amount of product*
in this reaction => $O_2$ is the limiting reactant. 
Compare mole ratio, 
$\frac{no.\ of\ moles\ of\ H_2O}{no.\ of\ moles\ of O_2} = \frac{2}{1}$ 
= no. of moles of $H_2O$ 
= $\frac{2}{1} \times 1$ 
= 2 mol

mass of $H_2O$ = no. of moles of $H_2O \times$ molar mass of $H_2O$ 
= $2 \times [(2\times1) + 16]$
= 36g

## Percentage Yield and Percentage Purity
yield => refers to the amount of product formed in a reaction

**Define theoretical yield:**
>[!definition]
>* Maximum quality of product that can be obtained calculated value from equation
#ChemistryDefinitions 


*In reality, amount of products produced experimentally produced in most reactions is always less than that produced theoretically (by calculation from balanced equation)*
WHY?
* use of reactants which are impure (contains impurity)
* Incomplete reaction as some reactants did not react completely
* Poor handling of reactants and products => when conducting experiments => which resulted loss of reactants or products.

**Define Actual Yield:**
* Actual quantity of product obtained (equal to or less than theoretical yield)

Actual yield < theoretical yield

**Percentage Yield => shows relationship between actual yield and theoretical yield**
*Formula:*
$$percentage/ yield/ of/ product = \frac{actual\ yield\ of\ product}{theoretical\ yield\ of\ product} \times 100\%$$
##### Worked Example 1
128g of sulfur dioxide, $SO_2$ was reacted with oxygen to produce Sulfur Trioxide, $SO_3$ 
148g of $SO_3$ was produced in the reaction. Calculate Percentage yield of $SO_3$ 

**Step 1** write balanced chemical equation for this reaction
(include state symbol)
$2SO_2(g) + O_2(g) \rightarrow 2SO_3(g)$ 

**Step 2** Identify the actual yield of the product which is provided in the stem of the question
Actual Yield of $SO_3$ = 140g

**Step 3** Calculate theoretical yield of product. 
No. of moles of $SO_2$ = $\frac{mass\ of\ SO_2}{molar\ mass\ of\ SO_2}$
= $\frac{128}{32 + (2\times16)}$
= 2mol

compare mol ratio, 
$\frac{no.\ of\ moles\ of\ SO_3}{no.\ of\ moles\ of\ SO_2} = \frac{2}{2}$
$\therefore$ of moles of $SO_3$ = mass of $SO_3$ 
= no. of moles of $SO_3 \times$ molar mass of $SO_3$ 
= 2 $\times$ 80
= 160g

**Step 4** Calculate percentage yield of product
Percentage yield of $SO_3$ = $\frac{actual\ yield\ of\ SO_3}{theoretical\ yield\ of\ SO_3} \times 100\%$ 
= $\frac{140}{60} \times 100\%$\
= 87.5

### Percentage Purity
Often => percentage yield of a product is less than 100% because reactants are not pure

*What is relationship between purity of the reactant and the percentage yield of a product*
* If purity % reactant increases, % yield of product % and vice versa

**Calculating Percentage Purity of a Substance**
$$ Percentage\ purity\ of\ substance = \frac{mass\ of\ pure\ substance\ in\ sample}{mass\ of\ sample} \times 100\%$$
=> if percentage purity of substance (sample) is 100% => we can deduce that the sample is pure

If percentage purity of the substance (sample) is less than 100% -> we can deduce that sample is impure

##### Worked Example
3.2g of impure impure copper was heated in air. 3.8g of copper(II)oxide was formed. 
i) what was the percentage purity of the copper sample
ii) what assumption was made when calculating the percentage purity of impure copper sample.

**Step 1** Write a balanced chemical equation for this reaction. 
(include state symbol)
$2Cu(g) + O_2(g) \rightarrow 2CuO(s)$

**Step 2** identify mass of sample in which is provided in the stem of the question
Mass of impure copper = 3.2g

**Step 3** Calculate mass of pure substance in sample.
$No.\ of\ moles\ of\ CuO = \frac{mass\ of\ CuO}{molar\ mass\ of\ CuO}$
= $\frac{3.8}{64+16}$ 
= 0.0475 mol

Compare mol ratio, 
$\frac{no.\ of\ moles\ of\ Cu}{no.\ of\ moles\ of CuO} = \frac{2}{2}$ 
$\therefore$ No. of moles of pure Cu = $\frac{2}{2} \times 0.0475$
= 0.0475 mol

Mass of pure Cu = no. of moles of Cu $\times$ molar mass of Cu
= $0.0475 \times 64$ 
= 3.04g

**Step 4** Calculate percentage purity of sample
Percentage purity of copper sample = $\frac{mass\ of\ pure\ copper\ in Cu\ Sample}{mass\ of| Cu\ Sample} \times 100\%$
= $\frac{3.04}{3.2} \times 100\$$
= 95.0%

*Assumption*: copper is the only substance that will react to give CuO. eg: if $CuCO_3$ was an impurity, heating could have given off CuO as well
