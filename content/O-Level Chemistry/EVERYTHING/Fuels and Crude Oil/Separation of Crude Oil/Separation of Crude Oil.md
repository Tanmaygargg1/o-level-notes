### Separation of Crude Oil by Fractional Distillation
Fractional distillation can be used to separate the crude oil into its various fractions because hydrocarbons present in crude oil have diff boiling points and are miscible.
[[Experimental Chemistry Main Note]]

>[!important]
>crude oil is separated into useful fractions by fractional distillation

___
school laboratory -> glass fractionating column with short glass tubes or glass rings is used for fractional distillation. 
- glass tubes / rings -> increase surface area for condensation in the fractional distillation process. 
- fraction with lowest boiling point is collected first -> following by fraction with next higher boiling point as heating continues. 

Fractional distillation of crude oil -> same process of boiling and condensation are involved -> but set up is diff. 

- For this industrial process -> diff fractions are condensed at diff points in a huge fractionating column.
- component with lowest boiling point is collected at topmost outlet and components with higher boiling points are collected at outlets down the column.
- set up allows all the fractions to be obtained continuously.

- Crude oil contains hydrocarbons with 1 to 70 carbon atoms per molecule.
- boiling points of hydrocarbons are related to the number of carbon atoms in each molecule. 
- <span style="background:#ff4d4f">hydrocarbons that contain more carbon atoms</span> -><span style="background:#ff4d4f"> have higher boiling points </span>
- hydrocarbons with lower boiling points are less *viscous* and burn more easily.

>[!important]
>each fraction DOES NOT have a fixed boiling point, it boils over a range of temperatures. 

>[!important]
>NOT ALL FRACTIONS EXIST AS GASSES. 

>[!important]
>- fractions at top have fewer hydrogen atoms than fractions at the bottom

___
#### Process
1. crude oil is first heated in furnace to about 400$\degree C$. boiling takes place. Vapour is passed into fractionating column

2. Fractionating column is hottest at bottom and gets cooler towards top. 
-> diff fractions condense at diff levels and are collected at respective outlets.
-> fractions with higher boiling points condense at higher temperatures and are collected at the lower levels of the fractionating column.
-> fractions with lower boiling points rise further up the column before they condense and are collected. 
____
diff uses are competing for non renewable supplies of crude oil.
