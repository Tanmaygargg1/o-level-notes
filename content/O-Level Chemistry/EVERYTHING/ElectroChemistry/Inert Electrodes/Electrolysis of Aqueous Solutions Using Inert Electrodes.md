in electrolysis - when more than 1 type of cation / anion -> is present in a solution -> only *one cation* and *one anion* -> is <span style="background:#d3f8b6">preferentially</span> discharged -> although both cations and anions would be attracted to cathode and anode respectively. 
- this is known as **selective discharge of ions**
___
there are more than 1 type of cations/ anions -> because $H_2O$ can also ionise -> and there will be hydrogen ions, $H^+$ and $OH^-$ ions from water. 
- so in electrolyte -> aqueous sodium chloride -> there will be 2 types of cations/positive anions and anions/negative ions. 

- $Na^+$ from salt and $H^+$ from water -> move towards cathode (negative electrode)
- $Cl^-$ from salt and $OH^-$ from water -> move towards anode (positive electrode)
(Unlike molten binary ionic compounds -> where this is only 1 type of cation and anion)
___
1. **Selective Discharge of Cations**
- metal ions of element -> lower in reactivity series[[Reactivity Series Main Note]] -> are discharged at cathode in preference to other cations in the solution -> since less energy is required. 
	- this is because -> cations of less reactive element gain electrons more readily -> thus greater tendency to undergo reduction. (position of ions is similar to metal reactivity series)

>[!summary]
>Cation that is selected for discharge ->less reactive -> has higher tendency to undergo reduction -> as it gains electrons more readily -> hence is preferentially discharged.
>
>As we go down reactivity series of metals -> the ease of discharge of cation increases

>[!important]
>metal above hydrogen - hydrogen ions preferentially discharged. vice versa


1. **Selective Discharge of Anions**
- For electrolytes that are <span style="background:#b1ffff">dilute aqueous solutions & concentrated solutions</span> -> that <span style="background:#b1ffff">do not contain halide ions</span> (negative ions from group 17, [[Group Properties - Group 17 Elements (Halogens)]])
- AKA for <span style="background:#b1ffff">solutions that contain sulfate and nitrate ions</span>, <span style="background:#b1ffff">regardless of concentration</span> -> hydroxide ions will be selectively discharged. 
	- Usually hydroxide ions ($OH^-$ ) are selected for discharge -> in preference to other anions (such as sulfate, nitrate, halide ions etc.) -> since less energy is required. 
	- This is because -> hydroxide ions -> lose electrons more readily -> thus greater tendency to undergo oxidation

- For electrolytes that are <span style="background:#b1ffff">concentrated solutions</span> -> that <span style="background:#b1ffff">contain halide ions</span> (negative ions from group 17 ([[Group Properties - Group 17 Elements (Halogens)]])
	- Halide ions are selected for discharge in preference to hydroxide ions -> in solution
	- This is because -> concentration of halide ions -> is higher -> than concentration of hydroxide ions.

- For electrolytes that are <span style="background:#b1ffff">dilute solutions</span> -> that <span style="background:#b1ffff">contain halide ions</span>
	- hydroxide ions will be selectively discharged in preference to halide ions <font color="#b2a2c7">based on the ease of discharge of anions. </font>


- [ ] In *concentrated* sodium chloride solution -> concentration of $Cl^-$ is higher than concentration of $OH^-$ -> as there is plenty of $NaCl$ -> $Cl^-$ will be preferentially discharged. 

- [ ] In *dilute* sodium chloride solution -> concentration of $OH^-$ -> is higher than concentration of $Cl^-$ because there is plenty of water -> $OH^-$ will be preferentially discharged as it loses electrons more readily -> thus greater tendency to undergo oxidation.

- Sulfates, Nitrates -> <mark style="background: #FF5582A6;">not discharged</mark> -> remain in the solution -> as more energy is required. they are not affected by concentration, be it high or low.
- less easily oxidised* 
- polyatomic anions**
