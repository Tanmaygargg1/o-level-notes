-> part of gas exchange process.
-> refer to muscular contractions and movement of ribs - which result in air moving in and out of the lungs.

humans possess special breathing mechanisms to increase rate of gas exchange between body and external environment.

2 phases:
Taking in of air - inhalation/inspiration
giving out of air - expiration/expiration
___
#### Thoracic (chest) Cavity

chest wall - supported by ribs
between ribs - 2 sets of muscles - external intercostal and internal intercostal muscles are found. 

-> antagonistic muscles - which mean that when external intercostal contract, internal intercostal will relax, and vice versa.

same information can be found in [[Parts of the Human Respiratory System#Ribs]]

thorax - separated from abdomen by dome shaped sheet called diaphragm:
-> [[Parts of the Human Respiratory System#Diaphragm]]
-> made up of elastic tissue and muscle.
-> when diaphragm muscles contract - it flattens and moves downwards
-> when diaphragm muscles relaxes - it expands and arches upwards.
workings of internal and external intercostal muscles change volume of thoracic cavity.

#### Inspired Vs Expired Air
some water -> evaporates from surfaces of alveoli
this explains why air we breathe out is saturated with water vapor

lungs -0 oxygen has been absorbed into bloodstream - CO2 is released from blood
-> air we breathe out has more CO2 than inspired air.

| Component      | Inspired Air                 | Expired Air                                |
| -------------- | ---------------------------- | ------------------------------------------ |
| Oxygen         | more                         | less                                       |
| Carbon dioxide | less                         | more                                       |
| Nitrogen       | same                         | same                                       |
| Water Vapor    | variable                     | saturated                                  |
| Dust particles | variable but usually present | little, if any                             |
| Temperature    | variable                     | about body temperature (37 degree celsius) |
