>[!definition]
>Anaerobic respiration is the release of energy by the breakdown of glucose in the absence of oxygen. Anaerobic respiration releases a relatively small amount of energy
> #BiologyDefinitions 

* in muscle cells
	* when little oxygen is available
	* happens for a short period of time
* maximum aerobic respiration is unable to release energy fast enough
* muscles incur <mark style="background: #BBFABBA6;">oxygen debt</mark>
* results in the formation of lactic acid and little energy*

*In Muscle Cells*
During vigorous muscle contractions -> muscle cells respire aerobically at a very high rate.
-> you may start panting in order to remove CO2 and take in O2 at a faster rate
-> heart may also begin to beat faster to transport oxygen to muscles at a faster rate
<mark style="background: #ADCCFFA6;">however there is a limit to increase in the rate of breathing and heartbeat. </mark>

When vigorous activity continues - muscular contractions become so vigorous -> that maximum aerobic respiration is unable to release energy fast enough to meet the demand.
- if vigorous muscle contractions continue - energy demand increases
- muscle cells carry out anaerobic respiration to meet increased energy demand
- <mark style="background: #CACFD9A6;">lactic acid</mark> is formed in the process.

Word equation for anaerobic respiration:
glucose -> lactic acid (this process releases a small amount of energy)
==glucose -> lactic acid + small amount of energy (exam phrasing)

Small amount of energy released during anaerobic respiration + energy released in aerobic respiration -> helps muscles to keep contracting

==Since there is insufficient oxygen to meet the demands of vigorous muscle contractions -> muscles are said to have incurred oxygen debt.

___
Anaerobic respiration produces lactic acid, which can lead to muscle cramps and fatigue. This buildup of lactic acid limits how long anaerobic respiration can continue. With regular training, your body becomes more efficient at clearing lactic acid and tolerating its effects, allowing you to perform anaerobic activities for longer before fatigue sets in.
