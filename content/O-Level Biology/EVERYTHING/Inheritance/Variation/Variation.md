>[!definition]
>Variation refers to the differences in traits between individuals of the same species
> #BiologyDefinitions 

- these traits show *discontinuous variation* -> which is controlled by one or a few genes. 
- in discontinuous variation -> there are clearly defined differences with few or no intermediate forms. 

Traits showing discontinuous variation: 
- traits show clear cut phenotypes with no intermediate forms between the traits. For example, in Mendel's experiment - seeds of pea plants were either green or yellow and either smooth or wrinkled. 
- traits are easily distinguished and are not affected by environmental conditions. Examples of such traits in humans are the ABO blood groups, double or single eyelids and ability to roll tongue.

___
Not all traits show discontinuous variation. 
Humans are not just dark or fair, tall or short. 

There are many intermediate skin colours and heights. such range of traits show *continuous variation*
Continuous variation is brought about by the combined or *addictive* effect of any genes. 

There are genes for dark skin and genes for fair skin. 
Continuous variation is also affected by env. conditions. 
E.g. greater exposure to sunlight may cause person to have darker skin. 
Other e.g. of traits that show continuous variation include height, show size, body type. 


| Discontinuous Variation                                                         | Continuous Variation                                      |
| ------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------- |
| Involves few clear cut phenotypes<br>(e.g. green or yellow seeds in pea plants) | Involves range of phenotypes.<br>(e.g. human skin colour) |
| Controlled by 1 or few genes                                                    | Controlled by many genes                                  |
| Genes do not show addictive effect                                              | Genes show addictive effect                               |
| relatively unaffected by environmental conditions                               | Greatly affected by env. conditions                       |
